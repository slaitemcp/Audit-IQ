ISO 9001:2026 — What's Changed
Published 16 September 2026 — replaces ISO 9001:2015 (sixth edition)
At a glance
•Published 16 September 2026, replacing ISO 9001:2015 as the current sixth edition
•Transition period of approximately three years — certificates to the 2015 edition remain valid to roughly September 2029
•An evolutionary revision: the 2015 framework, process approach, and Annex SL high-level structure all remain intact
Key changes
ClauseChange
5.1 LeadershipNew requirement for top management to explicitly promote and demonstrate quality culture, integrity, and ethical behaviour, with guidance on how this is evidenced — shared values, attitudes and observed behaviour, not just policy statements. The most significant conceptual addition in this revision.
5.2 Quality policyStrengthened to require the policy explicitly reflect organisational context, tightening the link to the Clause 4 context-of-the-organisation work already in place.
Clause 6 PlanningRisk- and opportunity-based thinking reinforced rather than restructured; clearer expectations on distinguishing and addressing risks and opportunities, and on planning changes.
Context / Clause 4Continued emphasis on climate change as a relevant issue when determining context, carrying forward what Amendment 1:2024 introduced.
Clause 3A limited number of new or clarified terms and definitions.
GeneralMinor terminology and clause-layout updates elsewhere, mostly for consistency and traceability rather than new obligations.
What this means in practice
•Existing ISO 9001:2015 certificates remain valid through the transition window — no immediate re-audit is triggered
•Certification bodies are expected to fold the new requirements into upcoming surveillance and recertification cycles rather than requiring a standalone transition audit
•Worth reviewing ahead of transition: evidence of leadership's promotion of quality culture and ethics, and quality policy wording against the strengthened Clause 5.2 requirement
Slaite Management Consultants & Projects and the AuditIQ platform track ISO standard revisions as they are published, so that audit methodology and criteria stay current with the latest requirements.
